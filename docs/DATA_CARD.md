# Data Card — Sol's TinyStories corpus

Every number below is printed by `data/eda.ipynb`'s final cell, sourced from
`data/processed/stats.json` and `data/tokenized/meta.json` — nothing here is
hand-typed or estimated. Regenerate the notebook
(`jupyter nbconvert --execute --to notebook --inplace data/eda.ipynb`) after
any change to the pipeline and update this file from its output, not the
other way around.

## Source

[TinyStories](https://huggingface.co/datasets/roneneldan/TinyStories)
(`roneneldan/TinyStories` on Hugging Face) — short children's stories
generated by GPT-3.5/GPT-4, restricted to a vocabulary a 3-4 year-old would
understand. Built by Eldan & Li (2023) specifically to make small-model
language training tractable. License: not formally specified by the dataset
authors; used here for non-commercial research/portfolio purposes only.

**Collection method:** entirely synthetic — LLM-generated, not scraped or
human-written. This matters for what the corpus can and can't teach a model
(see Limitations).

**Split used:** TinyStories' own native train/validation split, as held out
by the dataset authors, rather than a custom carve-out from one pool (see
`docs/PROJECT.md` → "Resolved decisions"). `config.data.val_split` in
`configs/micro_50m_8gb.yaml` is informational only for this dataset.

| **Kept** | **1,748,358** | **15,141** |
| Tokens (32k BPE) | **357,852,786** | 2,956,183 |

**Exact-dup rate: 14.58% train, 0.00% validation-internal — but 28.67% of raw
validation documents are verbatim duplicates of a train document.** That
second number is the interesting one: TinyStories' "held-out" validation
split is not text-independent from its 2.1M-story train split at the exact-string
level. This was caught and fixed mid-implementation — an earlier version of
`data/prepare.py` deduped train and validation against a single shared hash
set (train processed first), which silently absorbed every cross-split
duplicate into validation's own "exact_dup" counter before a separate
leakage-check pass ever ran, making that pass structurally report 0
regardless of the true rate (`tests/test_prepare.py` now pins the exact
before/after behavior as a regression test). The **outcome** — no document
text appears in both `train.jsonl` and `val.jsonl` as written — was already
true and independently verified
(`tests/test_pipeline_artifacts.py::test_val_disjoint_from_train_by_hash`,
which recomputes hashes from the actual files); only the *accounting* of
which bucket a dropped document belonged to was wrong.

**Practical implication:** perplexity measured on this validation split is a
weaker held-out signal than the "validation" label implies — nearly a third
of the documents that survive would, if leakage hadn't been filtered, have
let the model get credit for memorization rather than generalization. The
filtering removes that risk for what's kept; the leakage rate itself is
reported here rather than swept under the "held-out" label.

**Train's own dedup rate (14.58%)** is a real property of the corpus, not a
pipeline bug — TinyStories is LLM-generated from a constrained vocabulary and
prompt template, so verbatim repeats across "different" stories are expected.
Near-dedup (MinHash + LSH banding, `data/near_dedup.py`) is implemented and
unit-tested but was not run on the full corpus — the rate sits just under the
15% threshold that would have triggered it, and near-dedup on 1.75M documents
would materially extend runtime for marginal expected gain given the likely
cause (template reuse, not noise). Revisit if val loss underperforms target.

**Token cap, corrected:** `max_train_tokens` was originally a 400M target;
the actual cleaned corpus, encoded with the in-domain 32k BPE tokenizer,
yields 357,852,786 train tokens (~4.19 chars/token). The config was lowered
to this measured value rather than padded (see `configs/micro_50m_8gb.yaml`).

## Document length

| Percentile | Chars (train) | Tokens (train) |
|---|---|---|
| p50 | 778 | 184 |
| p90 | 1,210 | 298 |
| p99 | 1,863 | 479 |

**`block_size=512` is justified by measurement, not guesswork:** 99.74% of
train documents fit whole within 512 tokens (p99 = 479). The remaining 0.26%
get truncated during training — an accepted, quantified trade-off, not a
silent one.

Train and validation length distributions are close: two-sample
Kolmogorov-Smirnov test gives statistic 0.043 (chars) / 0.047 (tokens). The
p-values are tiny (~1e-25 to 1e-29), but at n=15,141 even small, practically
irrelevant gaps reach significance — the statistic itself, not the p-value,
is the number that matters here, and 0.04-0.05 indicates the two splits are
shaped alike. See `docs/figures/length_dist.png` (density-normalized, since
train outnumbers val ~115:1 by document count — raw-count overlay would
render val as a flat line).

## Tokenizer

32k-vocabulary byte-level BPE (`data/tokenizer/tokenizer.json`), trained on a
300,000-document subsample of **train only** — never validation, to avoid the
subtle leakage of a tokenizer's merge table reflecting eval-set word forms.
Specials: `<|endoftext|>` (id 0), `<|pad|>` (id 1), `<|unk|>` (id 2).

**UNK rate: 0.0000%** across all 357,852,786 encoded train tokens. Byte-level
BPE with a complete `initial_alphabet` (see `data/train_tokenizer.py`) is
lossless by construction — this number confirms that guarantee held at full
corpus scale, not just in the unit tests that first caught its absence.

Top-10 tokens by frequency: `.`, `Ġand`, `,`, `Ġthe`, `Ċ` (newline), `Ġto`,
`Ġa`, `Ġwas`, `Ġit`, `Ġher` — unsurprising high-frequency English function
words and punctuation. Frequency-rank plot in `docs/figures/zipf.png` tracks
the ideal Zipf slope (-1) closely through the first few hundred ranks, then
falls off faster than ideal in the long tail — expected for a small,
constrained-vocabulary corpus rather than open-domain text.

## Known biases and limitations

- **Vocabulary is deliberately narrow.** TinyStories was generated to use
  words a young child would know. Sol will not have exposure to technical,
  scientific, or domain-specific vocabulary.
- **Domain-locked to short children's stories.** No code, no math, no
  dialogue-heavy text, no non-narrative prose (lists, instructions, dialogue
  transcripts).
- **English only, no code-switching.**
- **Names skew US/Western-conventional** (LLM-generation artifact of the
  source models used to build TinyStories).
- **Synthetic, not human-written.** Any biases or stylistic tics of the
  generating LLMs (GPT-3.5/GPT-4 circa 2023) are inherited, not filtered out.
- **High template reuse** (14.58% exact-duplicate rate) — a byproduct of
  LLM generation from a fixed prompt template, not organic repetition in
  human writing.

## Intended use

Portfolio/research demonstration of from-scratch small-LM training. **Not**
intended for production use, factual question-answering, or any application
where the above biases would matter operationally.
